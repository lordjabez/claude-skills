# Feedback Examples

All of these are invented, about fictional people and situations. They show
the moves, not real content.

## Upward feedback letter

```text
Priya,

Another quarter gone, and I'm still looking forward to Mondays, which seems
worth a mention. Thank you for building a place where that's possible.

Your grasp of the customer is the reason this company works. It shows in how
plainly you explain our product to a skeptical buyer, in the way you ask for
the numbers before you tell the story, and in how calmly you handle an angry
account (and teach the rest of us to do likewise).

You also notice when a team is running hot. Twice this year you caught
burnout early and changed the plan before anyone had to ask. That's *uncommon*,
and it's a big part of why people stay.

That being said, you lean heavily on hallway conversations to move things
along. Those won't keep up once we double in size. I'd push you to add a few
mechanisms on top of the conversations, not instead of them: a short weekly
written update, a shared list of open decisions, or simply a follow-up email
after a meeting where something got agreed.

Part of this comes from a good instinct to spare people extra meetings, but
the side effect is that some of us find out late. When in doubt, send it to
more people than you think need it.

Also keep pushing yourself to hand things off completely. When you're in the
room, everyone defers to you, which is flattering and also a trap. I'm no
better at this than you are; I'll try to do it too.

I'm glad to help however is useful, whether that's drafting the weekly update
template or sitting in on a few handoffs. Just say the word.

Looking forward to the next stretch.

Jud
```

## Peer feedback, growth section

```text
Your attention to detail is also your biggest risk. It can turn into slow
releases (which I've only seen once or twice) and a reluctance to let others
sign off on things (which I see often). If the team is going to double next
year, others will need to own more of the review work. I can help, either by
pairing with the new leads or by writing a checklist generator for the common
cases, so just tell me which. I need to get better at trusting the other
managers' judgment too, so let's compare notes next month.
```

## Manager review of a report

```text
Strengths
- Deep database knowledge, steady under pressure
- Writes clear runbooks; this is uncommon! - **Raises the bar**
- Patient with new hires, good at explaining why - **Builds the team**

Development Opportunities
- Fixing things yourself is fast but doesn't spread the skill. Pair first!
  Let someone else drive. - **Develops others**
- Share the half-formed idea earlier, before it's polished

Manager support
- Protecting focus time
- Introductions to the platform group
```

## Self-assessment paragraph

```text
**Raising the bar** is something I try to do every week. When a launch goes
badly, I start with my own decisions (there's usually a clear one), then the
team's process, and only then the dependencies we couldn't control. The
rollback we fumbled in the spring traces back to a test plan I signed off on
without reading closely. I now read every one before approval, and I've asked
two others to check me on it.
```

## Accomplishment entries

```text
    Gathered requirements weekly from dispatchers, drivers, and the call center
    Replaced three duplicate alert services with one shared service
    Shipped to production in six weeks; 9,000 riders subscribed in the first month (triple the old opt-in rate)
    Led to a two-year extension of the contract

Log Shipper - small open-source tool for forwarding logs from embedded
devices to a hosted search service. Built for a lab with 300 sensors. 14 forks
and 40 stars (as of MM/DD/YYYY).

Support satisfaction - 92% this quarter, against a team average of 88%.
Slightly under the 95% goal, but the last three months ran 84%, 89%, and 92%
after the staffing change.
```
