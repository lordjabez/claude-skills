## Interim guidance

Until the analysis exists, treat formal technical writing as the core voice in
[SKILL.md](SKILL.md) with the casual register dialed down, and flag to him that
this mode is uncharacterized so he reviews the draft more closely than usual.

What almost certainly carries over:

- Brevity, and the refusal to pad. This is the most durable thing about his
  writing and there is no reason formality would undo it.
- Leading with the point, context after.
- Short paragraphs, one idea each, with lists used freely.
- Plain ownership of limitations and tradeoffs rather than overclaiming.
- Technical terms used without over-explanation for an audience that knows them.
- No corporate filler, no hype adjectives, no throat-clearing.
- Commas and parentheses over em dashes.

What to dial down or drop pending evidence:

- Casual markers: "y'all", "natch", "pretty dang close", "good times".
- Exclamation points.
- Sentence fragments for emphasis.
- Strikethrough humor.
- Allusive titles. Default to descriptive here and ask him if a formal piece
  wants a clever one.
- Cultural references. A Tolkien allusion may well be fine in a conference
  abstract and badly out of place in an RFP response, so ask rather than guess.

What to keep, carefully:

- One dry aside somewhere, if the document has any room for personality at all.
  His writing is rarely fully deadpan, and a completely humorless draft is
  probably wrong for him even in a formal register. Use judgment on audience.
